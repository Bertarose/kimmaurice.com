Topless at the spa: a rule for whom?

Can queer, trans and non-binary people, or anyone whose body doesn’t fit the boxes, enjoy a spa without covering their chest? Here is what the spas answered in writing, and what the others post on their websites.

Having to wear a swimsuit is one thing. Having to cover your chest is another.

What the spa wrote (translated from French)
“As a mainstream spa welcoming a varied clientele, including families and young children on certain mornings reserved for that purpose, our dress code is in line with the usual standards in Quebec.”
“[Can a man use the facilities topless, with only swim bottoms?] Yes.”
“[Can a woman do the same?] No, wearing a swimsuit top (or a one-piece swimsuit) is required.”
“In practice, each person is invited to wear swimwear (one-piece, two-piece or swim bottoms only) that matches their gender identity and their comfort, while respecting the family setting of the establishment (the upper body must be covered for people with a chest requiring a swimsuit top according to the usual standards in public places).”
“Our team also remains available, in complete discretion, at the front desk or by phone if a client wishes to get clarification before their visit.”
Spa Bolton · email of Nov. 17, 2026, in reply to the publication notice.
